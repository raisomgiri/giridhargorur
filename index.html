The problem

Your dashboard looks great. Your pipeline disagrees.

Most B2B marketing teams are busy. Fewer are accountable. These are the three gaps I see most often, and the ones I talk about most.

"Marketing sends leads. Sales ignores them."

Leads that never become deals

Volume goes up and conversion stays flat. The fix sits in qualification, scoring and the rhythm between the two teams.

"We need a CMO, but not full-time yet."

The methodology

Revenue Hydraulics™

B2B demand behaves like fluid in a closed system. It enters at defined points, moves only under pressure, loses energy to friction, and can be measured at every joint.

Engineers judge a fluid system by one question: where does the pressure go? I spent years marketing in fluid engineering, and the question stayed with me. Most marketing functions cannot answer it about their own pipeline.

Revenue Hydraulics treats marketing and sales as one engineered circuit. Every program is an inlet, a pump or a valve, and every unit of spend has to show up as flow at the other end. Four laws govern the system.

Law ISource

The Law of Source

Flow is only as good as its inlet.

Pipeline begins at named accounts and the buying groups inside them, never at anonymous form-fills. A committee of five people decides a B2B purchase, so the system is designed around the committee. Inlets are chosen before a single campaign runs.

What we measureShare of pipeline from target accounts · buying-group coverage per account
Law IIPressure

The Law of Pressure

Nothing moves without a pressure differential.

Buyers move when they see a gap between where they are and where they could be. A sharp point of view creates that gap. Volume does not: more emails at the same pressure only produce more noise.

What we measureEngagement depth per account · meetings per engaged account
Law IIIFriction

The Law of Friction

Pressure is lost in the middle.

Most pipeline does not die at the top of the funnel or at the close. It dies in the stretch between first interest and sales-ready, where no one owns it. The middle is instrumented first, and friction there is removed before more is poured in at the top.

What we measureMQL to SAL conversion · days between stages
Law IVClosed loop

The Law of the Closed Loop

A system that leaks cannot be measured.

Marketing and sales form one circuit. The handover is a valve, not an exit, and marketing keeps working the deal alongside sales. What comes out at revenue is read back to the source, so every rupee or dollar of spend can be traced to the flow it created.

What we measurePipeline and revenue per unit of spend · sourced and influenced revenue
